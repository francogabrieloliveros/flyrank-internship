## Case Study: Draw Collab

| AI Draft                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Edited Version                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Problem.** Artists hit a slump — running out of ideas, forgetting the fun in drawing, even as professionals. Drawing with others is a different kind of fun than drawing alone, so I built a free, no-turns, no-directions canvas to bring that back. <br><br> **What I Did and Decided.** I made it event-driven with Node.js and Socket.IO so drawing felt live instead of clicking to send, and stored every stroke in one array as the single source of truth, replaying it for new joiners and normalizing coordinates so every canvas stayed a 1:1 replica. Load testing found the real ceiling — stable at 50 users, crashing around 100 — and I chose to stop there instead of chasing scale I didn't need yet. <br><br> **What Came of It.** Me and my friends actually used it for fun, and the concurrency and 1:1 sync — the hardest part — works. The real flaw: drawings live in memory only with no database, so every server restart wipes the canvas, and I've just been careful to clear it myself before restarting. | **The Problem.** Artists hit a slump — running out of ideas, forgetting the fun in drawing — even as professionals. I wanted them to remeber the fun through drawing with others and interacting with them. <br><br> **What I Did and Decided.** I made an event-driven collaborative canvas with Node.js and Socket.IO to make drawing feel live. I stored every stroke in one array as the single source of truth, ensuring that users see the same 1:1 canvas wherever they are and whenever they join. <br><br> **What Came of It.** A collaborative drawing canvas that can handle 50+ users sending 2000+ drawing packets at 200ms latency. It was not flawless with no persistence yet, but it was enough for me and my friends to have fun. |

## Case Study: GLOFLEX

| AI Draft                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Edited Version                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Problem.** Not every person understands sign language, and the obvious fix — a camera — assumes the other person is holding one up every time they need to communicate. We wanted a glove instead, so the translation lives on the signer's hand and doesn't depend on anyone else's setup. <br><br> **What I Did and Decided.** Flex sensors alone could only tell us how bent a finger is, so I fused them with an accelerometer to capture the tilting and motion sign language actually needs. I built the hardware and wrote the threshold logic myself, encoding each gesture as a finger-bend array plus a hand-position value, and replaced our slow stacked if-statements with array-based matching to speed it up. <br><br> **What Came of It.** We hit 0.98s average latency and translated 26 of 37 gestures correctly — 73.7% accuracy, rated "Accurate" on our professor's rubric, and it won Best Research for our batch. The 11 that failed were similar-looking gestures our static calibration and shaky wiring couldn't reliably tell apart, which we accepted as a first-prototype tradeoff. | **The Problem.** Not every person understands sign language, so there's always a barrier between non-verbal and verbal speakers. Image processing is a good solution, but it wasn't convinient enough for us. <br><br> **What I Did and Decided.** We wanted a wearable, so we utilized flex sensors for finger bend detection and accelerometer for tilting and motion. I built the hardware and wrote the threshold logic, improving speed by replacing our slow deeply nested if-statements with array-based matching. <br><br> **What Came of It.** We achieved 0.98s average latency and translated 26 of 37 gestures correctly (73.7% accuracy) receiving a rating of"Accurate" and winning the Best Research Award. |
